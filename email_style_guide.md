# Email style guide for the Supervised Email Draft

Version: 0.2
Updated: 2026-11-06
Based on: 2 replies Zack sent to sales after a screening
Status: draft, awaiting Zack's review

This file holds writing rules only. It contains no email text, customer names or data.
It is revised as more of Zack's sent replies become available.

## Reader
One sales colleague who knows the customer but is not a chemist. They passed the customer's
data to Zack, often with questions of their own, and want to know whether Claros can treat
this water, what to tell the customer, and what to ask next. The full technical report is
available to them separately, so the email does not need to be complete.

## Seen in Zack's sent replies
Shape
- It is a reply inside an existing thread, so there is no subject line.
- Greeting: "Hi" or "Morning" and the colleague's first name.
- One opening sentence that sets the context: thanks for the materials, or what this note
  is (for example, a few thoughts ahead of a meeting).
- First paragraph: the verdict in plain words. Say how the water quality looks overall,
  whether the identified PFAS compounds are within Claros's treatment scope, and whether
  any compound raises a concern. A matrix issue gets one sentence that names it together
  with how it is handled (for example, an elevated parameter that can be managed by
  adjusting the reagent dose).
- Second paragraph: what still has to be clarified with the customer, and why it matters.
  Treatment objectives come first: flow rate and target effluent PFAS concentrations. When
  the colleague raised a question or made a distinction of their own, respond to it here.
- Closing: when the colleague is waiting for a go or no-go, give it in one sentence (move
  forward for now, or hold until a named detail is known). Then a short closing line.
  Sign off with "Best," and "Zack" on the next line, unless the note leads straight into a
  meeting, where a one-line closing is enough.
- About 80 to 90 words in both replies. Two or three short paragraphs.
Voice
- First person singular: "I did not identify", "I do not see", "I think", "I would recommend".
- Measured rather than absolute: "looks fine overall", "looks pretty good", "appear to be
  within our treatment scope", "should be manageable".
- Plain sentences. No headings, bullet lists, bold text, tables or status labels.
- Collegial and direct. No sales language and no filler.
- No numbers. Neither reply quoted a concentration, a flow or a percentage; the verdict is
  given in words.
What stays out
- Engine vocabulary: PROCEED, CONDITIONAL, CRITICAL, rule IDs, module names.
- The list of PFAS species with concentrations, category percentages and matrix tables.
- Disclaimers and footers.

## Assumed until more replies are available
- When something changes scope, cost or timeline, say it in one sentence: a compound Claros
  cannot treat, a need for pretreatment, a design flow above 100 GPM, an ultra-low target
  that needs analytical confirmation first, or samples that differ enough to affect the
  basis of design. This is the one case where a number may be quoted, and then only the
  one that makes the point.
- When there are several samples or a showstopper to explain, the email may run to about
  150 words.
- If values in the report still need checking against the source files, say so in one sentence.
