# Email style guide for the Supervised Email Draft

Version: 0.1
Updated: 2026-11-05
Based on: 1 reply Zack sent to sales after a screening
Status: draft, awaiting Zack's review

This file holds writing rules only. It contains no email text, customer names or data.
It is revised as more of Zack's sent replies become available.

## Reader

One sales colleague who knows the customer but is not a chemist. They want to know whether
Claros can treat this water and what to ask the customer next. The full technical report is
available to them separately, so the email does not need to be complete.

## Seen in Zack's sent replies

Shape
- It is a reply inside an existing thread, so there is no subject line.
- Greeting: "Hi" and the colleague's first name.
- One opening sentence that names the customer or opportunity and says what this note is.
- First paragraph: the verdict in plain words. Say whether the PFAS compounds and the water
  quality are within Claros's treatment scope, and whether any compound raises a concern.
- Second paragraph: what still has to be clarified with the customer, and why it matters.
  Treatment objectives come first: flow rate and target effluent PFAS concentrations.
- One short closing line.
- About 60 to 130 words. Two or three short paragraphs.

Voice
- First person singular: "I did not identify", "I think".
- Measured rather than absolute: "looks fine overall", "appear to be within our treatment scope".
- Plain sentences. No headings, bullet lists, bold text, tables or status labels.
- Collegial and direct. No sales language and no filler.

What stays out
- Engine vocabulary: PROCEED, CONDITIONAL, CRITICAL, rule IDs, module names.
- The list of PFAS species with concentrations, category percentages and matrix tables.
- Disclaimers and footers.

## Assumed until more replies are available

- Sign off with "Best," and "Zack" on the next line.
- When something changes scope, cost or timeline, say it in one sentence: a compound Claros
  cannot treat, a need for pretreatment, a design flow above 100 GPM, an ultra-low target
  that needs analytical confirmation first, or samples that differ enough to affect the
  basis of design.
- Use only the numbers that drive the verdict, at most three or four.
- When there are several samples or a showstopper to explain, the email may run to about
  180 words.
- If values in the report still need checking against the source files, say so in one sentence.
